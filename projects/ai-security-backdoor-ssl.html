Backdoor Attacks on Self-Supervised Models

A team research project investigating how patch-based backdoors can affect self-supervised learning and how feature-space analysis, filtering and retraining can reduce their impact.

ModelsBYOL · SimCLR · MSF · MoCo v2
DatasetsImageNet-10 · MS COCO-10
MetricsASR · clean performance
FocusAttack + defence
Research file01 Problem02 Workflow03 Defence04 Evaluation05 Contribution06 Lessons
01 / PROBLEM

Why backdoors matter in self-supervised learning

Self-supervised learning can learn useful representations from unlabelled data, but that same pipeline can be influenced by poisoned training samples. Our project tested whether hidden trigger patterns could change model behaviour while preserving apparently normal clean performance.

Research objective

Evaluate backdoor effectiveness and develop a repeatable defence pipeline that detects suspicious samples and reduces attack success with minimal impact on clean behaviour.

02 / EXPERIMENTAL WORKFLOW

From clean data to controlled attack

01
Prepare datasets

Built controlled ImageNet-10 and MS COCO-10 subsets and maintained clean/poisoned variants for comparable testing.

02
Inject a controlled trigger

Introduced a patch-based trigger into a limited portion of training data to simulate a stealthy poisoning scenario.

03
Train SSL models

Compared four self-supervised learning approaches: BYOL, SimCLR, MSF and MoCo v2.

04
Measure impact

Used Attack Success Rate alongside clean-performance metrics to distinguish attack effectiveness from general model degradation.

04 / EVALUATION

What we measured

4SSL model families compared
2dataset environments
ASRprimary attack-effectiveness signal

The useful part of the experiment was not only whether a backdoor worked, but whether the defence could lower attack success without destroying clean performance. The final evaluation compared attack and defence stages under the same experimental conditions.

05 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Security research as an engineering workflow

My work included dataset preparation and purification, controlled poisoning, model comparison, experimental testing and documenting results with the team. The project required repeatable scripts, test logs, shared milestones and regular supervisor updates rather than a one-off model run.

✓

Translated model behaviour into security-focused measures such as Attack Success Rate and clean performance.

✓

Worked across multiple SSL approaches instead of relying on one model family.

✓

Connected attack testing to a practical detection-and-mitigation pipeline.

06 / LESSONS

What this project changed for me

It showed me that AI security problems still need classic security habits: define the threat, create a controlled test, collect measurable evidence, separate detection from mitigation and make the experiment reproducible enough for another person to validate.
