Backdoor Attacks on Self-Supervised Models

Team research project examining how patch-based backdoors affect self-supervised learning and how filtering and retraining can reduce their impact.

ModelsBYOL · SimCLR · MSF · MoCo v2
DatasetsImageNet-10 · MS COCO-10
MetricsASR · clean performance
FocusAttack + defence
On this page01 Problem02 Workflow03 Defence04 Contribution
Problem

Backdoors in self-supervised learning

The project tested whether poisoned training samples containing hidden trigger patterns could influence model behaviour while preserving apparently normal clean performance.

Workflow

Controlled attack evaluation

01
Prepare datasets

Created controlled ImageNet-10 and MS COCO-10 subsets for comparable testing.

02
Poison training data

Introduced a patch-based trigger into a limited portion of the training data.

03
Compare SSL models

Evaluated BYOL, SimCLR, MSF and MoCo v2.

04
Measure impact

Used Attack Success Rate alongside clean-performance metrics.

Defence

Detect, filter and retrain

The defence pipeline used feature extraction and clustering to identify suspicious samples, remove likely poisoned candidates and retrain on cleaner data before re-evaluation.

Feature analysisInspect learned representation space
DBSCANCluster suspicious samples
FilteringRemove likely poisoned candidates
RetrainingMeasure whether ASR falls without excessive clean-performance loss
